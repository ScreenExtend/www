## The case in brief

1. **One template, stamped six times.** Every section is an H2, a one-line grey subhead, then a grid. The six use-case cards all have a Title Case noun-phrase heading, one 12-17 word sentence and then a 4-7 word kicker ("Or flip it." / "No cable, no extra hardware." / "Each one is its own display."). That long-then-short punchline beat is the clearest structural tell on the page.
2. **Tidy, parallel headings.** "Chat and email on the side / Meetings off your main screen / Docs while you work / Two screens anywhere / No Wi-Fi needed / More than one extra screen" reads like a list someone was asked to make parallel.
3. **Triads everywhere.** "Scan a code, type six digits, drag a window across"; "captures, encodes and sends"; "a recipe, a spec or a how-to"; "phone, tablet, or spare laptop"; the three hero chips; "Scale, orientation, refresh rate and video quality"; "Regenerate…, start…, set… and read…".
4. **The same reassurance, said again and again.** "Nothing to install" appears in the hero, the pairing subhead, FAQ 1 and the meta ("no client install"). "Works like any extra monitor" appears in the use-cases intro, step 4 and the FAQ. Writing the same thing four times is how a model pads copy.
5. **Defensive or fake-friendly lines.** "The real Add Device screen." "Set each device up the way it suits you." "use it however you like." "Yes." as the opener to a security answer.
6. **Brochure claims that would fit any product.** "Fast enough to work on." "keep the second screen smooth." "Two screens anywhere."
7. **Jargon for a non-technical reader.** WebRTC, client install, encodes, 90th percentile, NVENC/AMF/Quick Sync/VideoToolbox/Media Foundation, 20H1/20H2, virtual display, extended monitor, hosted network, debug logs, dashboard, .exe/.dmg.
8. **Colon and semicolon cadence.** "Anything with a modern web browser: …", "Experimental: built, but…", "ARM build: not available yet.", "As many as you like; each one…", "Settings stay on your device; a session…", "…over WebRTC; free, with…".

What's already human: the Windows and Mac install notices (Download.tsx:157-217) are specific, ordered and plain, and they read as if someone actually clicked through them. Leave them mostly alone. Step 3 ("Your computer now treats the other device as a second monitor, sized to fit it.") is also good.

## Unclear or untrue-sounding (pull-out list)

- **"No Wi-Fi needed"** (UseCases.tsx:28) contradicts the FAQ, which tells you to join a Wi-Fi network. It should say "No router needed".
- **"Windows 10 (2020)"** (HeroLanding.tsx:60) reads as a wrong date.
- **"None is stored. Settings stay on your device"** (FaqSimple.tsx:31) contradicts itself, and it doesn't say which device.
- **Resolution question answered with scale** (FaqSimple.tsx:18-19). It reads as dodging the question.
- **"the dashboard"** (FaqSimple.tsx:27) is a name used nowhere else on the page.
- **"The real Add Device screen."** (ConnectFlow.tsx:69) invites the question "were the others fake?"
- **"use any device"** in the page title (seo.ts:4, index.html:8) is an overclaim. It needs a modern browser, and the FAQ already says so. Suggest "use your tablet or phone as a wireless second monitor".
- **"captures, encodes and sends each frame in under 7 ms"** is honest only with the footnote, and the footnote is in jargon. A reader may take 7 ms as the total delay. The rewrite in row 3 makes clear that the network adds to it.
