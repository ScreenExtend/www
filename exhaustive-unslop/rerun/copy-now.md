What you can use it for

Drag any window onto it, the same way you would with a plugged-in monitor.

Chat and email on the side

Keep Slack, Discord or your inbox on the tablet and give your work the whole main screen.

Meetings off your main screen

Put the call on the tablet and keep taking notes and working on your computer. Or flip it.

Docs while you work

A recipe, a spec or a how-to stays in view instead of hiding behind a tab.

Two screens anywhere

Your laptop and the tablet already in your bag make a two-screen setup. You don't need a cable.

No router needed

On Windows, ScreenExtend can start its own Wi-Fi network, so the tablet connects straight to your computer.

More than one extra screen

Connect a phone and a tablet at the same time. Each one is its own display.

Quick on your end. Your computer captures and encodes each frame in about 8 ms.* Your Wi-Fi and the other device add their own delay on top of that.

*Median on real 1080p content, with 9 in 10 frames under 10 ms. Measured on Windows 11 (25H2) with an NVIDIA RTX 3080 Ti Laptop GPU.

Like Apple's Sidecar, but for any device with a browser

Sidecar turns an iPad into a second screen for a Mac. ScreenExtend does the same job for a Windows PC or a Mac, and the second screen can be an Android tablet, an iPad, a phone or an old laptop.

Pick something else if…

You only use a Mac and an iPad.

Sidecar is already on your Mac and works over a cable too. Use it.

You want a cable between a Windows PC and a tablet.

Spacedesk (free for personal use) and Duet (paid) both work over USB.

You mostly want to play games on the other screen.

Moonlight with Sunshine, or Parsec, are built for games and support HDR. ScreenExtend doesn't do HDR yet.

You'll sit at the same desk every day.

A basic 24-inch monitor costs about $90. It will look sharper and react faster than any wireless screen, ours included.

You want one keyboard for two computers.

That's a different job. Universal Control and Deskflow share a keyboard and mouse; they don't add a screen.

Try ScreenExtend when you want an extra screen from a device you already own, on Windows or a Mac, for free.

Frequently asked questions

Not covered here? Email support@screenextend.app.

Which devices can I use as the second screen?

Anything with a modern web browser: an iPad or Android tablet, a phone, or another laptop. Nothing gets installed on it. ScreenExtend itself runs on your Windows PC or Mac.

How do I use ScreenExtend?

Run it on your computer, scan the QR code (or open the link) on the other device, and type the 6-digit code. That device becomes an extra monitor.

Is my data secure?

The picture and sound are always encrypted. A device can only join with the session's 6-digit code, and you can make a new code any time to keep others out. The join page itself loads over plain HTTP on your own network unless you switch on the HTTPS version, which your browser may warn about because the app makes its own certificate.

What resolution and refresh rate can it do? Can it game?

Each device can go up to 200% scale, and you can set its refresh rate anywhere from 15 to 550 Hz. In our tests the computer sent about 150 frames a second; what you actually see depends on the other device and your Wi-Fi. Gaming works if your network keeps up. HDR is coming in a future release.

How many devices can connect at once?

Several. Each one gets its own display, and each one you add uses more of your computer. With NVIDIA graphics, up to 8 devices use the graphics chip; any more fall back to the processor.

How does offline mode work?

On Windows, open Settings and start the hosted network with a name and password. Join that Wi-Fi on the other device and open the link ScreenExtend shows. No router needed. This isn't available on Mac yet.

What data does ScreenExtend collect?

Your screen is never stored. When a device joins over the internet, our relay passes a hashed IP address and a rough region to your computer, and our server keeps short-lived service logs. The Privacy Policy has the details.
